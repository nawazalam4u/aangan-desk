1. Automation brief

Pain

About 200 enquiries a month, and roughly 48% get no reply within 48 hours (around 96 a month). A third arrive outside the 10am to 7pm desk. Nikhil's own numbers: an answer inside an hour converts at 4× the next-day rate, and the average project is ₹8–14 lakh. When an enquiry does reach a designer it is a forwarded message with no context, so the first call is spent re-asking what the desk already asked.

September phone evidence: T08 missed at 10:47pm and the call-back went unanswered; T16 called on Monday and was never logged; T07 hung up before anyone took a name or number; T17's line dropped.

User

The caller: a first-time Pune or PCMC enquirer who talks to the assistant. The designer: 14 of them, mostly out on sites, who receive the note. Nikhil: owns the rules and reads the dashboard.

The front desk (2 people) is not replaced. They keep consultations, calendars and relationships; the assistant removes the unanswered and unrecorded calls.

Outcome

  • 100% of calls answered in under 5 minutes, day or night (target: on the first ring)
  • 0 calls lost to process (missed, unlogged, no details)
  • Every qualified lead reaches a designer in under 5 minutes with all the basics
  • 0 prices quoted
  • Cost per qualified lead on the dashboard

The journey today (phone)

  1. A caller rings the studio number, often after seeing Instagram or a friend's flat.
  2. 10am to 7pm only. Outside that the phone rings out (T08, 10:47pm: no voicemail, call-back next morning unanswered).
  3. The desk asks questions in whatever order comes to mind. Size, timeline and ownership are asked in some calls and skipped in others.
  4. Notes are inconsistent. T16: "I don't see a note", the Monday call was never recorded. T07: no name or number taken.
  5. The desk decides alone who is "worth a designer", with Nikhil's rubric in their head (Nashik, advice-only, restaurant, ₹1–1.5 lakh budget were all handled well, but only by memory).
  6. The lead is forwarded to a designer as a message with no context; booking waits for the calendar ("I'll revert by end of day", T12).
  7. The designer calls and re-asks the same questions, and Nikhil has no number for any of it until he pulls them by hand.

2. The Nine Checks

Evidence is from the case files and the 20 phone transcripts.

Kill switches: any No means do not build
01

Problem Real

Is there documented, ongoing evidence of pain?

Pass
Nikhil's own pull: 48% unanswered in 48 hours and a 4× conversion gap. The transcripts show it live: T08, T16, W02, W08 (a customer says "is anyone monitoring this number?").
02

Workflow Repeated

Does this task recur regularly?

Pass
~200 enquiries a month, with 20 phone calls in the 40-transcript September sample. The same five questions are asked on nearly every call.
03

Input Available

Do all inputs exist and are they accessible now?

Pass
Yes for the logic: 40 transcripts, services.md, qualified.md and pricing.md exist today. Caveat: the live phone line (a number, telephony and speech) is an integration, not data, so it is not in the demo. The demo uses the browser's microphone.
Sizing: any No means build something smaller
04

Output Valuable

Would someone pay for or act on this output?

Pass
A designer acts on a complete note; Nikhil acts on the dashboard. Callers get an answer at 11pm instead of nothing.
05

Impact Measurable

Can you track improvement with a number?

Pass
Logged on every call: time to answer, outcome, time to Telegram, time to designer claim, minutes, AI cost. The dashboard replaces Nikhil's manual pull.
08

ROI Worth It

Does the benefit clearly outweigh build and running cost?

Pass
The AI itself costs an estimated 40 paise a call (the dashboard shows the measured figure once real calls run); with an assumed ₹10 a minute for the phone and speech layer the total is roughly ₹46 a call, about ₹9,000 a month at 200 calls. One extra ₹8–14 lakh project covers about a year of it. The voice price is an assumption to replace with a real quote.
Boundary: any No means build the tool, the human stays here
06

Failure Risk OK

If the AI gets it wrong, what is the consequence?

No for pricing
Pricing fails this check. A wrong number on a ₹8–14 lakh decision anchors the customer and cannot be taken back. T13: a standard vs premium kitchen alone can be 3× the cost. Every other failure is recoverable (a wrongly closed caller is logged and reviewable), so those stay with the AI.
07

Judgment Protected

Does a human review before anything consequential happens?

Pass
The assistant only forwards. The designer owns the price, the consultation and the fit; senior staff own complaints (T09); nothing is booked, promised or quoted by the AI.
09

Owner Clear

Is one person accountable for the output?

Pass
Nikhil. He owns qualified.md, the rules in code mirror it, and the dashboard lists every closed call so he can spot a good lead declined by mistake.

The Cut: quoting per-square-foot pricing

Nikhil asked for it: "the system should be able to quote our standard per-square-foot pricing so people don't have to wait for that."

The check that stops it: 06, Failure Risk (with 07 behind it). pricing.md is headed "indicative only, varies by site" and says no number from the guide should ever be quoted. It lists what the agent must never say: any figure, range, "it'll cost around", "our rates start at". The only approved answer is to defer to the designer and offer the booking.

What I built instead: the assistant answers a price question with the approved line, once, and keeps going. It never disqualifies for asking (qualified.md). Two September callers (T02, T13) asked twice, were deflected, and still booked. That is the evidence this works.

Enforced in code, not only in a prompt: every reply passes a filter that blocks any rupee figure, "lakh", "per sq ft" and similar. If the AI ever slips, the reply is replaced with the approved line and the dashboard counts the attempt.

What Nikhil gets for his underlying need (people should not wait): the caller is answered in seconds and the designer calls back with a real number at the consultation.

Why the AI is a small box: it turns speech into facts and facts into a friendly sentence. Deciding who is worth a designer's time, and whether a number can be said, is plain code written from Nikhil's own rubric, so I can test it against all 40 real enquiries (they agree with my reading of his rubric on all 40; “qualified” and “needs one more answer” count together because a live call asks the missing question) and he can change a rule without touching a prompt.

Telegram or email for the handoff?

I chose Telegram, and kept the database as the permanent record.

Telegram (chosen)

  • It matches the 5-minute promise. A push alert reaches a designer standing on a site. Email gets triaged later.
  • One tap to own it. The "I'll take it" button stops two designers calling the same person, and the claim time is measured on the dashboard.
  • No routing guesswork. We have no data on designer specialties, so a shared group beats picking one inbox.
  • Free and instant. A bot is a minute to create; no domain, SPF or spam-folder risk.
  • I have already built this pattern in Case 1.

Email

  • Good record and attachments, but easy to miss on a phone.
  • No claim button, so duplicate or dropped follow-ups.
  • Needs a verified sending domain to stay out of spam.
  • Weakness of Telegram I am covering: messages can be deleted and are not a system of record, so every note also lives in the database and the dashboard. The group must be private: it contains callers' phone numbers.

When I would switch: if designers will not install Telegram (many Indian teams live on WhatsApp, but its business API needs paid, pre-approved templates), or if compliance needs email. The sender is one function, so the change is small.

Red flags I found, and what I did

HIGH

The ask contradicts pricing.md

Nikhil wants per-sq-ft quotes; his own guide forbids any number. Not built; deflection is enforced in code. See The Cut.

HIGH

A real phone line is not included

"Day or night" needs an Indian phone number, telephony and speech in English, Hindi and Marathi (W03 is Hinglish). That needs accounts, KYC and money only Nikhil can provide. The demo runs the full brain through the browser mic; connecting a provider replaces only the voice layer. Next step, not a hidden gap.

MED

Two timeline rules disagree

services.md says execution cannot start if it must be ready in under 6 weeks; qualified.md says 8–10 weeks. I used 6 weeks as the hard stop and flag anything under 12 weeks for the designer. Nikhil should confirm.

MED

A 500 sq ft commercial minimum appears only in T18

The desk said it on the call, but it is not in services.md. Implemented as a soft rule in one constant; Nikhil to confirm or delete.

MED

The rubric has no category for angry existing clients

T09 (a client with an unreplied designer) is not an enquiry. I added an escalation path that skips qualification and alerts a senior person. The AI never argues or promises.

MED

Recording and consent

The greeting says it is an AI and that the call is recorded. India's data-protection law applies to callers' phone numbers; the Telegram group must be private and members limited to designers.

MED

The 5-minute promise stops at Telegram

If no designer claims a lead, it still waits. The dashboard measures claim time; the next step is an automatic nudge to Nikhil after 15 minutes unclaimed.

LOW

"4× conversion" is a correlation

Fast answers may go to hotter leads. I will not promise Nikhil a 4× lift; the dashboard lets him measure the real effect.

LOW

Small data problems

The brief names "Arjun" (the founder is Nikhil, a leftover from Case 02). The zip had no enquiries/ folder; the 40 transcripts came as one PDF and I used all of them. Phone numbers are redacted, so repeat-caller matching is tested on the caller-ID field only.

Where HubSpot fits

HubSpot is the CRM: the system of record for each lead after the call. The assistant creates the lead and writes it into HubSpot as a contact (matched on phone number, so a repeat caller is the same record), with the handoff note on its timeline, a call-back task, and a deal in the first pipeline stage. From there the designers and Nikhil work the relationship in HubSpot, and move the deal toward won or lost.

Our own database and dashboard answer a different question: how is the phone assistant performing? (volume, speed, cost). HubSpot answers: what is happening with each customer? The split is deliberate. Not-a-fit callers are never pushed into the CRM, no price is ever written to it, and if HubSpot is down the call, the Telegram note and the dashboard still work. The connection is checked live on the How it connects page.

How this extends to WhatsApp and the web form

Not built (out of scope), but the design is ready: the rules engine, the guardrail, the note and the dashboard are channel-free. A WhatsApp message would call the same /api/turn endpoint and the web form is a single-shot version of the same extraction. I already ran all 40 enquiries, including the 10 WhatsApp and 10 form ones, through the rules as a test. Only the trigger and the reply channel change. The September numbers show why it matters next: 2 of 10 WhatsApp threads sat unanswered for 2 to 4 days, 5 more waited 16+ hours, and the form enquiries have no response times recorded at all.
